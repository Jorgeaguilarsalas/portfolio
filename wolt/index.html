From single-use to circular operations — reducing courier equipment procurement spend by 30% across 6 German cities. The program did not make gear cheaper. It made gear stop leaving. Recovery at offboarding, refurbishment in the Berlin office, and a downstream recycling route for what could not be saved turned a per-hire purchase into a pool that circulates.

01 · Signal

Equipment was consumable by default.

Every new hire at Wolt received a full gear kit — thermal bags, weatherproof jackets, thermal boxes — imported from overseas manufacturers and distributed across Germany. Departing couriers kept theirs. The cost curve tracked hires and turnover, not active courier count.

Starting conditions

  • 2,000+ courier partners across 6 German cities. Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Düsseldorf and Stuttgart, each with its own local operations team.
  • Equipment lifecycle: single-use per courier. A kit was issued once, at onboarding, and was not expected back.
  • Scope: nationwide. Wolt Germany operations, coordinated from the Berlin office.

02 · Diagnosis

Three systemic gaps in the gear lifecycle.

The problem wasn't per-unit gear cost. It was the assumption that gear was single-use — reflected in three operational gaps.

  1. No structured recovery at offboarding. Departing couriers walked away with their gear. There was no return channel, no accountability, and no incentive to bring it back.
  2. No financial mechanism to enforce return. Missing gear generated no visible cost signal — neither to the courier nor to the company.
  3. No downstream path for damaged gear. Even when items came back, worn or irreparable pieces had no recycling channel. They went to landfill.

03 · Decision

Redesign the lifecycle from linear to circular.

Instead of optimizing gear procurement or reducing new-hire kit cost per unit, we redesigned the full lifecycle. The gear should not leave the system when a courier left the system.

  1. Offboarding recovery. Structured gear return at exit, integrated into the offboarding checklist.
  2. Financial accountability. Missing items charged to the courier at offboarding — a behaviour-change mechanism, not a revenue generator.
  3. Warehouse refurbishment. Central intake in the Berlin office: condition assessment, cleaning, minor repair, and a re-issue pool for new hires.
  4. Downstream recycling. Irreparable gear routed to Wolt HQ in Denmark via the existing partnership with TARPREC, which processes bags and heating plates in Tørring for material recovery.

The loop, drawn

Four stages that keep gear inside the system, and one downstream route for what cannot be saved.

Procurement stops being the engine of the system and becomes its residual input: new units are bought only for the demand the returned pool cannot cover. Everything the lime path touches is equipment that was already paid for once.

Gear that leaves with a courier is not a logistics problem. It is a design decision nobody made on purpose.

04 · Operation

A cross-functional system across national scale.

A loop only exists once four functions run it. The system required coordination across warehouse, support, local operations and HQ — in six cities and two countries.

  • Warehouse operations (Berlin office). Designed the intake workflow, condition assessment criteria, and cleaning and repair protocols. Coordinated a small team of Gear Associates for daily execution.
  • Offboarding integration. Worked with Support and Ops teams to embed gear return into standard offboarding procedures across the 6 German cities.
  • Nationwide rollout. Coordinated implementation across Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Düsseldorf and Stuttgart with local ops teams.
  • HQ Denmark coordination. Aligned with Wolt HQ on the downstream material flow: consolidation of irreparable gear, shipping logistics to the TARPREC facility in Tørring, and the material recovery pipeline.
  • Intake & grading workflow
  • Offboarding checklist
  • Cross-border material flow

Carbon footprint quantification

The program's environmental effect was quantified rather than asserted, so it could be published externally and audited against the material flows it described. That calculation is what turned an internal cost programme into a reportable sustainability contribution.

05 · Result

The cost curve decoupled from turnover.

First 12 months 30%↓ Equipment procurement spend, first 12 months at Wolt (Feb 2021 – Jan 2022) baseline vs. next 12 months post-implementation (Feb 2022 – Jan 2023)
Nationwide 6 cities Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Düsseldorf, Stuttgart
Scale 2,000+ Courier partners covered under the circular gear system

The decoupling was structural, not incidental. Once the recovery, refurbishment and recycling loop was operational, new-hire kits increasingly consisted of refurbished gear from the returned pool. Procurement cost stopped tracking hire volume.

The program's environmental contribution — quantified for external publication — fed into Wolt's broader sustainability reporting, contributing to the 25,000+ kg of material diverted from landfill via TARPREC across Wolt's European operations (source: Wolt Blog, October 2023).

06 · Next iteration

What I'd add if I were running this today.

Three things I did not have in scope but would build next.

  1. Predictive gear allocation. Match refurbished inventory to the hire pipeline more actively, reducing residual procurement variance.
  2. Component-level circularity. Beyond bag and jacket recovery, track individual component wear — straps, zippers, insulation — for targeted refurbishment.
  3. Multi-market benchmark. Extend the framework to other Wolt geographies where scale and turnover profile would justify similar programs.
