02 · Featured case study

Bliq — Rebuilding fleet supply in a market that already had incumbents.

Drivers and fleets already belonged to Uber and Bolt. Incentives bought activation and never bought retention: fleets switched on for a bonus, and switched back when it ended. I designed an Earnings-per-Hour model with a service-level agreement attached, piloted with one fleet before it went near the network.

  • The fleet insight. Operators wanted predictability, not bigger bonuses — short campaigns made vehicle planning impossible, so the model committed to earnings across the low season.
  • SLA as the mechanism. The guarantee was conditional: >90% acceptance, ≤10% cancellations and availability at peak, so subsidised supply became actual capacity.
  • Attribution. I owned the pilot design, the SLA, the budget and the rollout call; the 4.6× driver growth over the period is a company-level result the pilot contributed to.

Jul 2024 — Jul 2025 · Supply Operations Manager · 12 months

04 · Featured case study

Wolt — Turning courier gear from a purchase into a loop.

Courier equipment at Wolt Germany was bought once per hire and never came back: the cost curve tracked hiring and turnover rather than active courier count. I redesigned the lifecycle from linear to circular — recovery at offboarding, refurbishment in Berlin, and a recycling route for gear that could not be saved.

  • Lifecycle redesign. Four intervention points: offboarding recovery, financial accountability for missing items, warehouse refurbishment, and downstream recycling via TARPREC through Wolt HQ Denmark.
  • Nationwide rollout. Gear return embedded into offboarding across 6 German cities covering 2,000+ courier partners.
  • Result. 30% reduction in equipment procurement spend, first 12 months against the following 12 post-implementation.

2021 — 2022 · Operations Associate · Nationwide

05 · Featured case study

Bolt — Rebuilding a driver activation funnel nobody could see inside.

Driver activation at Bolt Rides in Southern Germany was a single number reported weekly: around 25%, with no visibility into which step drivers stalled at. I built the in-app instrumentation to see the funnel stage by stage, then designed interventions against each specific leak.

  • Instrumentation first. In-app analytics measuring drop-off per stage — registration, upload, verification, activation, first ride — replacing a weekly aggregate that hid the leak.
  • Diagnosis. Five friction points: manual document review, generic communication, duplicate account blocks, language barriers, and deprioritised long-tail fleets.
  • Result. Activation moved from 25% to 58% in six weeks across 1,000+ registrations.

2023 · Operations Specialist · 6 weeks

06 · Featured case study

Alcorte Alemán — Running a 50-year-old butcher shop through WhatsApp.

An AI order-management system for a family butcher shop in Mexico City, designed around a single constraint: the operator never opens a dashboard. WhatsApp is not a channel bolted onto the product — it is the entire product surface, for customers and owner alike.

  • Constraint-driven architecture. “No dashboard” treated as the specification rather than a limitation to work around.
  • Production integration. Meta WhatsApp Business API, Vercel, Supabase RLS and a Claude agentic loop with tool calling, wired end to end against live data.
  • Scale. 120+ products across 1000+ customers, with row-level security keyed on vendor_id so the schema is multi-tenant from day one.

2025 — Present · Side project · Solo build
